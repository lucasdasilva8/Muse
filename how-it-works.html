Process

How Muse works

Three sides of the same offer: the artist sets terms, Muse verifies and structures pricing, fans fund and get paid from defined revenue.

For artists

From application to payouts

01

Apply & verify identity

Stage name, links, raise purpose. Muse confirms who controls the listing.

02

Traction + financials

Monthly listeners and growth, plus disclosed revenue backed by documents.

03

Set offer terms

Choose raise (R), share % (S), term (T), and optional cap (C). You control how much you’re offering.

04

Listing, raise, report, pay

After approval, fans fund the raise. You report defined net on schedule; Muse routes the fan pool’s share.

For fans

What you buy into

01

Read the listing

Terms, verification level, included income categories, fees, and risk — before you pay.

02

Invest an amount

Your fraction of the fan pool is what you invest divided by the total raise.

03

Receive payouts

Each period, the pool gets S × defined net. You get your fraction of that pool, until the term or cap ends.

Pricing structure

Simple math, no black box

Muse suggests a coherent raise from disclosed revenue, verification quality, traction, and the terms the artist chooses.

Symbol Meaning
R Raise amount — capital the artist wants to raise
S Revenue share — portion of defined net sold to the fan pool
T Term length in months
C Optional return cap (e.g. 1.5× total invested)
V_ann Estimated annual defined net from disclosure + docs
Q Revenue quality — higher when documents verify the numbers
G Traction factor — listeners, growth, release activity (bounded)
V_adj = V_ann × Q × G
R_suggested = (S × V_adj × T/12) / C
V_implied = R / S
fan_fraction = amount_invested / R
period_payout = fan_fraction × (S × defined_net_period)

Payouts stop when the term ends or when the fan pool has received C × R (if a cap is set).

This is a planning framework, not a promise of returns. Music revenue is uncertain. You can lose money. Live investing requires legal review.
